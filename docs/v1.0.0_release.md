# AegisDrive-V v1.0.0 — integrated engineering assurance release

**Integrated Automotive Safety, Security, MBSE and V-Model Engineering Assurance Case Study.**
Educational research artifact, November 1, 2026 (America/New_York). No certification,
homologation, production ECU readiness, accredited independent assessment, ISO compliance
or AUTOSAR SecOC conformance is claimed. Same automation acts in separate review roles;
no external board or hidden third-party validation is implied. Unresolved hazards are an
explicit part of release acceptance, not filtered from the metrics.

## What changed and why

v0.4.0 was frozen and audited before control changes. Its source manifest had no unexpected
changes, all 92 tests passed, official model checks reproduced and all 846 original
non-host-timing outputs matched. All eighteen strict/recovery security hold-out collisions
have individual sensor-to-impact timelines. Their evidence-backed grouping is strict B:
six stale-delivery, four counter-gap, two restart/rollback; recovery C: the same six stale
cases. Every matched A case avoids collision, showing withholding plus rejection/timeout
availability cost. Zero logical crypto execution delay prevents attributing them to host CPU.

The candidate adds only scoped newest-authenticated route arbitration and corrected
current-pressure/100 ms clear-dwell diagnostics. No MAC, sender, age or replay gate is
bypassed; no unauthenticated/stale command is accepted for availability. Shared-clock/key/
ECU/actuator assumptions and both-path loss remain. Native ECU core/C ABI source is unchanged
from v0.4.0, verified byte-for-byte. Default fusion remains the legacy union policy because
controlled corroboration/temporal/confidence/capped-braking alternatives expose true-obstacle
failures, persistent ghosts and other tradeoffs. Study policies are explicitly opt-in.

## Robustness findings

v0.4.0 and candidate both collide in **47/108** final stratified cases; each has **9/108**
achieved unnecessary-braking cases under the narrow no-target definition. Actual v0.3
collides in **37/81** supported common cases. Compare only the common 81-case subset when
assessing version differences; the security-specific denominator is different. The new
route-selection contract is exercised but no aggregate collision reduction is demonstrated.
The intentional strata contain shared ECU, actuator, low-grip, short-gap and ODD failures;
these counts are not real-world collision probabilities and receive no inappropriate
binomial aggregate interval. Relative impact speeds are reported, not injury severity or
crash energy without a mass model. No following-vehicle dynamics are present.

Existing regressions remain: **29/120** prior physical hold-outs, **12/48 strict** and
**6/48 recovery** security collisions. Previously exposed sets are not called unseen.
Conditional illustrative Wilson intervals and exact denominators accompany them, with
sampling limits. New strata and seeds were fixed before final outcomes and were not used
to tune thresholds. Native rejection accuracy is distinguished from semantic safe intent
and availability; correct-key compromise and ghosts remain explicit counterexamples.

The 200-run study shows brief ghost suppression by temporal confirmation, but persistent
common ghosts survive. Corroboration/confidence suppression can collide with genuine targets,
and even the nominal temporal profile has a collision seed. The 3 m/s² study reduces intensity
but fails some close-target cases. No default false-positive improvement is claimed by hiding
those false negatives. An earlier confidence-gate study error is preserved/corrected explicitly.
