# AEB safety analysis — provisional engineering assessment

This is a simulation safety argument, not an ISO 26262 compliance or certification claim.
All numerical limits, classifications and safety requirements below are project proposals.
No normative ISO clauses or requirements are asserted. Independent domain review, field exposure
data and vehicle validation are absent; ASIL proposals must not be used for real vehicle allocation.

## Item definition and boundaries

The item assists an enabled passenger-car driver in longitudinal rear-end threats on a straight,
flat road, one relevant in-path passenger vehicle, activation 10–80 km/h. It contains radar,
ADAS (decision and supervision), CAN, Brake ECU (validation, arbitration, simulated actuator),
and BCM (lamps and warning). The driver, lead vehicle, road friction, vehicle motion and electrical
power are external dependencies. Radar delivers range/rate, health, presence and sample time;
ego speed has independent validity/time inputs. HMI enables the function; driver braking wins
when its demand is greater. No steering avoidance, lane association, cut-ins, pedestrians,
road grade, following traffic, hydraulic faults or power faults are modelled.

Modes remain DISABLED, STANDBY, MONITORING, WARNING, BRAKING, DEGRADED, FAULT.
Initial self-reported radar health is not detection confidence. Measured target correctness,
object identity and false-positive rates are unassured. Nominal dry friction remains the ODD;
wet/very-low friction experiments do not expand it. Shared monotonic time, 10 ms scheduling,
20 Hz radar, 10 ms deterministic CAN delivery and 0.15 s actuator time constant are assumptions.
The driver does not react before collision and holds the vehicle after a completed stop.

## Technical safety concept, root causes and alternatives

**FI01:** frozen acquisition timestamp exceeds 150 ms; ADAS disables demand, correctly avoiding
continued reliance on stale evidence. No independent sensor exists and the threat appears after
freeze. Safety expectation SG-01 availability cannot be met with this architecture. Keep stale
rejection/alert; propose independent camera/radar with separate power/timing, validated object
association, confidence and disagreement handling. A second simulated copy of the same radar
would not establish independence. Braking on missing data can itself cause HE-02; no universal
emergency fallback is implemented. Existing braking also releases on stale inputs: this remains
an unresolved tradeoff requiring trajectory/rear-threat analysis and a bounded fallback concept.

**FI02:** Brake ECU clears AEB after >100 ms without an accepted command; ADAS formerly had no
return path. Now the C++ supervisor independently validates 0x220 CRC/counter/payload, accepted
command counter progress, reported timeout/rejection and brake-active response. Initial sync
is not treated as data. Body receives the same broadcast without consuming it for ADAS.
An unavailable indication is latched until disable/re-enable. Requests continue when fresh
perception supports them; feedback loss alone creates no braking demand. Single command-path
loss still prevents braking: diagnosis cannot replace actuation. A redundant command path,
independent supervisor actuation authority and a bounded last-valid-command hold require a
separate hazard tradeoff. Indefinite hold can cause unnecessary braking; immediate timeout
release can cause collision. Current 100 ms release policy is retained and explicitly open.

Acknowledgment is the last **accepted** 4-bit request counter, not status transmit liveness.
0x220 byte 5 bit 0 is ack-valid; byte 6 low nibble the counter; other new bits remain reserved.
A changing echo refreshes progress. This detects sustained loss/stuck echo under 10 ms receipt,
but is not a unique command identity, cryptographic freshness or proof of achieved deceleration.
The existing reported actuator output is upstream of plant friction saturation; it is not a
wheel/IMU measurement. Active-bit supervision detects gross nonactuation, not under-delivery
or sufficient stopping performance. Those remain open design limitations.

**S02 / wet road:** constant-velocity TTC ignores future lead deceleration and grows too late
at high speed. Added envelope D = margin + 0.35*v + v²/(2*a_plan) - v_lead²/(2*8), with
v_lead=max(0,v+range_rate), a_plan=4 m/s² by default. Intervention needs a closing speed >0.5
m/s and valid input, as well as the unchanged activation window. Original TTC remains an OR
condition and original requested-deceleration clamp is retained. The 0.35 s allowance covers
assumed sample/network/actuator response conservatively for these experiments; no general
stopping guarantee follows. The constant lead bound 8 m/s², flat road and available ego
braking >=4 m/s² are engineering assumptions, not sensed facts. No ground-truth mu is passed
to ADAS. At mu=0.3 the ego cannot achieve this planning deceleration; collisions remain.
Earlier activation can increase false braking and comfort costs: target selection/confidence
and further traffic studies are required before any real deployment.

**S04:** instantaneous range rate becomes opening under ego braking despite continued lead
slowing. Driver model reaccelerates after release. Stopping envelope alone still allowed
retrigger in the first candidate. Added filtered lead-speed derivative (0.8 old + 0.2 new,
only new timestamps, reset on invalid/absent data) as an additional release guard. Its -0.3
m/s² threshold is an experiment assumption and noise-sensitive; no target identity tracker
exists. Sustained clearing is required, not a permanent braking latch. S04 and true clearing
unit tests both run. Object disappearance still releases after 500 ms; occlusion versus
true departure cannot currently be distinguished.

## Focused FTA (qualitative)

Top event T1 = rear-end impact despite enabled AEB. OR branches:
A insufficient valid threat information (sensor freeze OR plausible missed target OR ODD exclusion);
B insufficient delivered deceleration (command path loss OR actuator failure OR adhesion below
assumption OR request late/weak);
C premature release followed by renewed closing.
Under the specified inattentive-driver model each branch can independently cause T1; these
are qualitative candidate single-point cut sets, not quantified probabilities. Avoidance
would require BOTH timely justified demand AND enough delivered braking AND maintained safe
separation. T2 = harmful unnecessary braking: false perception OR accepted malicious/invalid
demand OR excessive hold after evidence loss; driver/follower exposure determines harm.
CRC detects accidental corruption but is no protection against an attacker recomputing it.
Common causes: power, scheduler, time source, CAN, and correlated sensors. Neither fault-tree
independence nor diagnostic coverage has been established. See `models/fault_tree.puml`.

## Review priorities

Independent safety review of S/E/C and ASIL proposals; bounded fallback/FTTI study; redundant
perception and actuation; friction/achieved-deceleration feedback; confidence and object
association; false-positive, occlusion, driver override and target-switch scenarios; real CAN
arbitration and timing; security authentication; physical lamp monitoring. Existing tests are
simulation evidence only, not proof of safety goals or certification.
